The Overlay Backfires on FTSE 250 Under Realistic Costs
net return · the plain, unscaled Black-Litterman portfolio beats every single overlay variant
Top-50 Ensemble is the star performer under academic costs on FTSE 250 (14.2–14.7% net, Sharpe 0.77–0.80) — the opposite ranking from FTSE 100, where it was the weakest ML strategy. This confirms the ranking signal has genuine edge in the mid-cap universe, at least before realistic frictions are applied.
Realistic costs erase that edge entirely. Top-50 Ensemble flips to −3.1% to −3.5% net, and ML-BL falls to −5.4% to −5.6% — still deeply negative even with the volatility overlay helping (the unscaled ML-BL loss was −15%). Only 1/N (5.4–6.3%) and Min-Var (3.0–3.2%) stay positive.
The real headline: doing nothing beats the overlay. The plain unscaled Black-Litterman portfolio, with no volatility scaling applied at all, returns 8.3% net (Sharpe 0.40) — higher than any realistic-cost overlay result on this index, including ML-BL's best overlay outcome (−5.4%). On FTSE 250, the overlay's extra notional exposure (mean s > 1) adds enough borrow and trading cost that the risk-timing benefit is outweighed. The lesson: the volatility overlay is not a free lunch, and its net benefit is capacity/liquidity-dependent — it worked cleanly on FTSE 100 (large, liquid names) but backfires on the costlier FTSE 250 mid-caps.
